# Introduction

Farrier is an open-source, agentless machine identity automation platform. It discovers, manages and secures the machine identities across your infrastructure: TLS certificates, SSH keys, cryptographic assets and post-quantum (PQC) readiness. Your team oversees; Farrier handles the rest.

Nothing is installed on the machines it manages. Farrier works over the network: TLS handshakes, SSH, and each device's management API.

## What it manages

- **TLS certificates.** It finds what your endpoints actually serve, plans renewals into change windows, issues certificates on the device's behalf, deploys them (or hands a person a change pack), and only calls a renewal done when a handshake shows the new certificate live. This includes the machines ACME can't reach: load balancers, firewalls, VPN concentrators, Java keystores and vendor appliances.
- **SSH keys.** Host keys and algorithms from a scan, and `authorized_keys` from each host. Weak and unknown keys are flagged and can be removed. See [SSH keys](/docs/ssh).
- **Cryptography and PQC readiness.** Protocol versions, ciphers and key exchange for every TLS and SSH service, a post-quantum status for each, and a CycloneDX CBOM. See [Crypto and PQC readiness](/docs/pqc).
- **Tickets and events.** ServiceNow and Jira tickets that follow each manual change, and events to Datadog and Splunk. See [Integrations](/docs/integrations).

## The problem

The CA/Browser Forum's ballot SC-081 shortens the maximum lifetime of public TLS certificates in steps:

| From | Max lifetime | Domain-validation reuse |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Sep 2020 | 398 days | 398 days |
| 15 Mar 2026 | 200 days | 200 days |
| 15 Mar 2027 | 100 days | 100 days |
| 15 Mar 2029 | 47 days | 10 days |

Cloud workloads already auto-renew with certbot, cert-manager or their platform. The pain is the long tail: anything that needs an API call to a management plane, a keystore rewrite and restart, or a ticket and a human in a maintenance window. At 47 days, an estate of 1,000 certificates with 40% installed by hand needs about 47 renewals every working day and more than two full-time engineers. Run `farrier forecast` to see your own numbers.

## How Farrier closes the gap

The device never talks to a CA. Farrier completes ACME DNS-01 validation through your DNS (or uses its built-in private CA), then:

- **Automated connectors** push the certificate to F5 BIG-IP, FortiGate, PAN-OS, Java keystores and any host with files over SSH. Each install goes in under a new name, switches over, and records how to undo it.
- **The manual connector** builds a change pack (PEM, PFX, a device-specific runbook) and can open a ServiceNow or Jira ticket. The renewal closes itself when the scanner sees the new certificate live.
- **Verification** is a real handshake. If an automated deploy never shows the new fingerprint, Farrier rolls back.

## Who it's for

Infrastructure, network, PKI and security teams who own a mixed estate of appliances, servers and legacy services, and who need machine identities handled in a way that respects change control instead of bypassing it.

<Callout type="warning" title="Project status">
Every commit runs the renewal engine's tests, each connector against its vendor's documented management API, and the ACME client against Let's Encrypt's Pebble test CA; Let's Encrypt staging and AWS ACM run nightly. Before Farrier changes production, run the [pilot checklist](/docs/pilot) on one endpoint per device type, as with any certificate automation rollout.
</Callout>
